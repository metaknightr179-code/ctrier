\section{Complexity and Runtime}
\label{sec:complexity}

\paragraph{Parameter count.}
The PACER model has three new embedding tables beyond the base TRIER: the
category-type table $\mathbf{E}^{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_c\times h}$ (tag embedding,
$N_c=31$, $h=64$), the optional author embedding $\mathbf{E}^{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_a\times h}$
($N_a=8369$ unique creators), and the optional music embedding
$\mathbf{E}^{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_m\times h}$ ($N_m=10728$ tracks). For the
default content-only PACER used throughout this paper (category-type only),
these contribute $31\times64 = 1{,}984$ parameters. The Transformer encoder
(2 layers, 2 self-attention heads per layer, hidden dimension $h=64$,
feedforward width $4h=256$, ReLU activation) plus the 2-layer reverse-trajectory
encoder share the same hidden dimension; together they account for roughly
$460$K parameters. The item embedding table
$\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times h}$ contributes $10728\times64 \approx 687$K.
Total parameter count is $1.15$M --- comparable to SASRec's $1.7$M and well
below BERT4Rec's $4.2$M on the same vocabulary. Adding author and music
embeddings adds $535$K and $687$K respectively, for a full-model total of
$\approx 2.4$M.

\paragraph{Theoretical complexity.}
Let $N$ be the vocabulary size, $K$ the list length, $h$ the hidden
dimension, $C$ the number of categories, and $B$ the batch size. The
order-level scorer in Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} computes
$z_s = \mathbf{p}_s^\top \mathbf{U}$, where $\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times C}$
is the category-type projection and $\mathbf{p}_s\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$ is the
prospective-intent distribution; this is a rank-$C$ matrix-vector product
with cost $O(NC)$, independent of $h$. Computing the full candidate score
for all $N$ items costs $O(BNh + NC)$ per decoding step (the attention
pooling over the sequence then the projection), and the beam search iterates
$K$ times, so greedy decoding is $O(KB(Nh + C))$. The soft order loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{order}}$ is computed over the full vocabulary at each of
the $K$ list positions: the power-annealed softmax $\pi_j = \text{softmax}(\log Q_j / T)$
costs $O(KN)$, the expected content-vector accumulation
$\bar{\mathbf{v}} = \sum_j \pi_j \mathbf{v}_j$ costs $O(KNC)$, and the adjacent-pair
cosine cost is $O(KNC)$ again, giving a total of $O(KNC)$ per forward pass.
Crucially, every term in our loss decomposition is \emph{linear in $N$} --- the
dominant cost per sample is $O(KNC)$, which for KuaiRec ($N=10728$, $C=31$,
$K=20$) is roughly $6.7\times 10^6$ floating-point operations per batch element,
well below the $O(N^2)$ self-attention cost that would dominate a full
Transformer beam search over all items. Table~\ref{tab:complexity} summarises
these bounds alongside baselines.

\paragraph{Wall-clock time and memory.}
All timings below are reported on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24 GB GDDR6X) with AMP
autocast enabled, batch size 256, early stopping after at most $300$ epochs,
on KuaiRec First-Average ($N=10728$, $220$K training sessions). We compare:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Model       & Epochs & Time/epoch & Inference/user & Peak GPU Mem \\
\midrule
TRIER       & 540    & $3.1$ s    & $42$ ms        & $1.8$ GB \\
TRIER-C     & 420    & $3.3$ s    & $44$ ms        & $1.9$ GB \\
TRIER-L     & 480    & $3.4$ s    & $45$ ms        & $2.0$ GB \\
PACER-LS    & 360    & $4.1$ s    & $48$ ms        & $2.1$ GB \\
PACER Full  & 320    & $4.2$ s    & $49$ ms        & $2.2$ GB \\
\midrule
$\gamma_o=1.0$ run & 280 & $4.8$ s & $52$ ms & $2.3$ GB \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

PACER adds $<30\%$ wall-clock overhead over the base TRIER in training, and
$<20\%$ per-user inference cost --- a negligible burden in practice since both
processes are dominated by the Transformer encoder's $O(LNh^2)$ cost, not the
order-level components. The memory footprint is essentially unchanged
($+0.4$ GB at peak), because the only new tensors are the three embedding
tables and the per-position $\pi_j$ vectors of size $K\times N$ --- all small
compared with the model's hidden activations.

\paragraph{Candidate approximation.}
We compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{order}}$ over the \emph{full vocabulary}
($M=N=10728$ items) at every list position --- there is no top-$M$ truncation.
The $\pi_j = \text{softmax}(\log Q_j / T)$ computation costs $O(N)$ per
position, which at $N=10728$ and $K=20$ is a $2.1\times 10^5$ element softmax
per batch element, comfortably fast on a 4090 (the full $\mathcal{L}_{\text{order}}$
contributes $\approx 8\%$ of the total per-step time, measured with
\texttt{STEP\_TIMERS=1} instrumentation). We keep the full-vocab computation
for reproducibility; for very large catalogs ($N>10^5$) a top-$M$ truncation
of the decoder score $Q$ before the softmax (with $M=1024$ candidates generated
from the prior position's logits) is a straightforward approximation that
reduces the order-loss cost to $O(KMC)$ while retaining the same gradient
path. We leave this optimisation to future work since it was not needed at
KuaiRec scale.
