\subsection{Implementation Details}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{KuaiRec}~\cite{kuairec} is a fully observed real-world short-video recommendation dataset with dense engagement logs; each video is annotated with one of 31 semantic categories. Following the engagement aggregation protocol of~\cite{kuairec}, we construct four variants\footnote{The user and item counts are identical across the four variants; the interaction count shown is for the \emph{individual} variants ($\approx$9.55M for the \emph{average} variants).} according to whether positive engagement is determined by the \emph{individual} viewing ratio or the video-\emph{average} viewing ratio, and whether the \emph{first} or \emph{highest} engagement of each user--item pair is retained: \emph{highest\_individual}, \emph{highest\_average}, \emph{first\_individual}, and \emph{first\_average}.
    \item \textbf{MovieLens-1M (ML1M)}~\cite{harper2015} is a classic movie rating dataset. We treat ratings of at least 4 stars as positive feedback and apply iterative 5-core filtering on users and items. Movies are described by 18 multi-label genres that serve as categories.
    \item \textbf{MicroLens}~\cite{ni2023microlens} is a content-driven micro-video recommendation dataset with rich per-video category metadata. We retain interactions whose watch-time-to-duration ratio is at least 0.1 and deduplicate user--video pairs, keeping the highest-ratio engagement. Videos are organized into 57 content categories.
    \item \textbf{KuaiRand-1K}~\cite{gao2022kuairand} is an unbiased short-video recommendation log collected under randomized exposure. We keep only fully watched (\emph{long-view}) interactions, deduplicate user--video pairs, retain the 20,000 most popular videos, and apply iterative 5-core filtering. Videos carry 44 content tags.
\end{itemize}

For all datasets, items are re-indexed to $1,\ldots,|\mathcal{I}|$ with $0$ reserved for padding, and interactions are grouped by user and sorted chronologically into sequences. We adopt the leave-one-out protocol: the last item is the test item, the second-to-last the validation item, and the rest form training. All sequences are truncated to a maximum length of 50.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocol}
We evaluate all models by ranking the held-out item against the \emph{entire item set} without sampled negatives, avoiding the inconsistencies of sampled metrics~\cite{chen2021}. We report accuracy metrics Recall@$k$ (Hit Ratio), NDCG@$k$, and MRR@$k$; diversity metrics Intra-List Distance (ILD@$k$)~\cite{zhang2022} and Category Coverage (CC@$k$)~\cite{zhou2018}; and the Consecutive Similarity (CS@$k$) of adjacent list items, for $k\in\{5,10,20\}$. ILD is the average pairwise Euclidean distance between L2-normalized item vectors, CC the fraction of categories covered by the list, and CS the average cosine similarity between items at consecutive positions (lower CS means locally less repetitive lists). Crucially, ILD and CS are measured for \emph{all} models in the same frozen, pretrained content-embedding space (the dataset-specific category/item2vec vectors released with the data), so cross-model diversity numbers are directly comparable.

\subsubsection{Model Configuration}
Both the RT and PT encoders use $d=64$ hidden units, 2 transformer layers, and 2 attention heads, with dropout 0.5 on embeddings and transformer sub-layers; parameters are initialized from $\mathcal{N}(0,0.02^2)$. We use Adam~\cite{kingma2015} with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and batch size 256, train for at most 1{,}000 epochs with early stopping on validation performance (patience 100, minimum improvement $10^{-4}$). The RT model is trained first to convergence and frozen when training PT. We set $K=5$ retrospectively generated items, beam width $M=3$, the diversity trade-off $\lambda=0.1$, and the consecutive-penalty weight $\lambda_c=0.01$; $\lambda$ and $\lambda_c$ can be adjusted at inference without retraining. All baselines share the same split, full-catalog protocol, Adam ($10^{-3}$, batch 256, max length 50), and early-stopping schedule (500 epochs, patience 100); their architecture hyperparameters follow the respective original papers and are selected on the validation set.

\subsubsection{Baselines}
We compare with representative sequential recommendation methods, grouped into (i) vanilla sequential recommenders, (ii) self-supervised sequential recommenders, and (iii) diversified sequential recommenders.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GRU4Rec}~\cite{hidasi2016}: A GRU-based recurrent recommender that models user sequences with gated recurrent units.
    \item \textbf{SASRec}~\cite{kang2018}: A causal self-attentive sequential recommender that captures sequential patterns with a unidirectional transformer.
    \item \textbf{BERT4Rec}~\cite{sun2019}: A bidirectional transformer trained with masked item prediction for contextual sequence modeling.
    \item \textbf{DuoRec}~\cite{wang2022duorec}: A self-supervised transformer combining dropout-based model-level augmentation with supervised positive-pair contrastive regularization; it is also the backbone architecture of the PT module.
    \item \textbf{TRIER}~\cite{shi2024trier}: The diversified sequential recommender most directly related to PACER and its foundation. A retrospective transformer reverse-predicts plausible historical interactions and produces $M$ augmented sequences via beam search, after which a prospective transformer generates lists that cover the reconstructed intents with an intent-coverage diversity loss. We run the official implementation under our unified split and full-ranking protocol, using plain item-ID embeddings and no content fusion, dense supervision, or position-aware consecutive penalty, so any gain of PACER isolates the contribution of our extensions.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Ablation Variants}
To disentangle the contributions of PACER's components, we additionally report: (i)~\textbf{PT-no-consec}, which keeps the intent-coverage loss ($\lambda=0.1$) but disables the consecutive-similarity penalty ($\lambda_c=0$), testing whether local position-aware smoothing is needed on top of global intent coverage; (ii)~\textbf{PACER-full}, with both losses enabled at $\lambda=0.1$, $\lambda_c=0.01$; and (iii)~\textbf{RT-only}, the retrospective model evaluated alone for next-item prediction, assessing the standalone value of reverse trajectory modeling.
